# The circuits of Bravyi et al.'s public code, as used by "Fail fast" (2026-11-03)

arXiv:2511.15177 (Beverland, Carroll, Cross, Yoder) analyzes the memory circuits generated by
github.com/sbravyi/BivariateBicycleCodes (decoder_setup.py, commit fa77e33): the depth-8 cycle of arXiv:2308.07915
with its standard schedule, d noisy cycles and noise-free ones, standard circuit noise. For Z-type noise its Table 2
gives D <= 10 for BB(12) (gross) and D <= 18 for BB(18) (two-gross), the latter from 1,981,650 logical operators of
weight 18 found by search, and it counts the minimum-weight failures (9 faults for BB(18)) from those operators.

`papers/bravyi_repo_circuit.py` rebuilds these circuits in Stim; `papers/bravyi_repo_check.py` runs decoder_setup.py
itself (imports of ldpc and bposd replaced by a stand-in that supplies the same logical operators) and compares the
columns of its decoding matrices HX and HZ, with logical rows, against the circuit's detector error model:

| record | code, cycles | columns Z basis (HX) | columns X basis (HZ) | equal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `check_bb72_c3.json` | [[72,12,6]], 3 | 1,188 | 1,152 | yes |
| `check_gross_c3.json` | [[144,12,12]], 3 | 2,376 | 2,304 | yes |
| `check_bb288_c2.json` | [[288,12,18]], 2 | 3,312 | 3,168 | yes |

Certified distances (`papers/bravyi_repo_circuit.py KEY BASIS CYCLES OUT [kmax] [seconds per threshold]`, pipeline of
`papers/run_parallel.py`, `dp` not used). Basis Z = Z-basis memory = X-type noise; basis X = Z-type noise, the type
Table 2 reports.

| record | code, cycles | basis | lower | upper | time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `gross_c12_Z.json` | gross, 12 | Z | 10 | 10 | 10 s, 6 threads (Mac) |
| `gross_c12_X.json` | gross, 12 | X | 10 | 10 | 38 s, 4 threads (Mac) |
| `bb288_c18_Z.json` | two-gross, 18 | Z | 16 | 16 | 6,160 s, 24 threads (5090c) |
| `bb288_c18_X.json` | two-gross, 18 | X | 13 | 16 | 584 s, 24 threads, 300 s per threshold (5090c) |
| `topdown_bravyi_bb288_r18_X_W15.json` | two-gross, 18 | X | 16 | 16 | 9,245 s, 48 threads (5090) |

The two-gross X record has a fault set of weight 16 found by the one-round window search and replayed in Stim
(no detector fires, observables 0, 1, 2, 6, 7, 11 flip); its sector search at weight 13 hit the 300 s limit. An earlier
run without a limit (`bb288_c18_X_untimed.log`) had not finished weight 13 after 70 minutes on 24 threads and was
stopped. The two-gross Z witness (weight 16) also replays (observables 0, 1, 4, 5, 8, 10).

Lifting through weight 15 closes the X basis (2026-11-04, `~/claude_runs/bravyi_z` on 5090, same circuit, sha256
971dd47e...). `scripts/enum_collapsed.py CIRCUIT enum_bravyi_bb288_r18_X.json 15 3 0 bravyi_repo_circuit:bb288`
enumerated every solution of the time-collapsed problem (collapsed distance 12) up to weight 15: 48, 1,728, 40,464 and
754,464 solutions of weights 12 to 15 in 1, 12, 283 and 5,250 orbits (9,103 s). The translations are the 144 of the
code, from its check permutations (`bravyi_repo_circuit:bb288`); the detector coordinates show only 12 of them.
`scripts/run_topdown.py CIRCUIT enum_bravyi_bb288_r18_X.json 15 topdown_bravyi_bb288_r18_X_W15.json bravyi_repo_circuit:bb288`
then searched the circuit's sector problem (2,880 x 26,208) for a fault set of weight <= 15 over each orbit: none
(142 s), so 16 is certified (`topdown_bravyi_bb288_r18_X_W15.json`, kernel sha256 7fbf93d0..., as `cdist/gmitm.c`;
logs `*_bravyi_bb288_r18_X*.log`).

So the distance is exactly 16 for both noise types, not 18, and the minimum-weight failures have 8 faults, not 9.
The gross code's 10 is exact in both bases.

Note: `circuits/bb*_r*.stim` are a different construction of IBM's schedule (CZ gates, SI1000-like noise, from the
tesseract-decoder files); the audit's [[288,12,18]] row was certified on those. These records certify the authors'
own construction.
