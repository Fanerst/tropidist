## Construction and check matrices

Locators: Sec. III B, Eqs. (11), (15), Theorem III.2 / Eq. (19); Sec. IV A, Eqs. (26)–(28); Sec. IV B, Eq. (42); Sec. IV F 2, Eqs. (90)–(91); Sec. IV G, Eqs. (106)–(107); Sec. VI B, Eq. (116); App. B, Eq. (B2); Table I caption.

- **Cells.** These are the points `z=(a,b)` of the finite periodic lattice `Z_Lx × Z_Ly` (the quotient by `x^Lx+1, y^Ly+1`, Eqs. (90)–(91)). Each cell holds one data qubit of block L and one of block R, so `n = 2·Lx·Ly`.
- **Group action.** The space group is `G = T ⋊ P`. An element `g = t_b p_ρ` acts on cells by `z ↦ ρz + b` (Eq. (27)). The generators are `t_x`, `t_y` (unit translations), `s_x: (a,b) ↦ (−a,b)` and `s_y: (a,b) ↦ (a,−b)` (Eq. (42); App. B Eq. (B2)), so mirrors pass through the origin. The n=96 row of Table I uses a skew mirror `s: (x,y) ↦ (x,−x−y) mod (24,2)` (Table I caption).
- **Words.** A word such as `t_x s_y t_y` is a composite map whose rightmost factor acts first. This is consistent with the paper's example in Sec. IV G, where `s_x s_y t_y^{-1}` sends `xy` to `x^{-1}`.
- **Checks.** Eq. (15) sets `H_X = (Γ(f_X) | Γ(h_X))` and `H_Z = (Γ(f_Z) | Γ(h_Z))`, where Γ is the permutation representation of Eq. (11). Every code in the tables uses `f_X=f^T, h_X=h^T, f_Z=h, h_Z=f` (Eq. (116)). Row `p` of `Γ(f^T)` is then the support `f(p) = {g(p): g∈f}`, which gives the generators of Eq. (107):
  - X check `p`: L block `f(p)`, R block `h(p)`;
  - Z check `p`: L block `h^T(p) = {g^{-1}(p): g∈h}`, R block `f^T(p)`.
  
  Coincident terms cancel mod 2. The paper remarks below Eq. (107) on the extra transpose that this convention introduces.
- **Commutation.** The checks commute iff `Γ(fh + hf) = 0` (Theorem III.2). This is automatic for BB codes, but for space-group codes it is a real constraint on the search, because `f` and `h` need not commute in `F_2[G]`.
- **Lattice size.** Table I gives the group as `(Z_Lx × Z_Ly) ⋊ P`, which fixes `(Lx, Ly)` in that order. The transposed order fails for every non-square row except [[12,8,2]], where 3×2 also gives k=8 but the label says 2×3. Table II gives the shape `Lx × Ly` explicitly. Table III gives no shape (see the ambiguities section).

### The two n=112 rows: two-block group-algebra codes

Table I labels [[112,6,12]] and [[112,16,10]] with `(Z_7 × Z_4) ⋊ C_s`. That lattice has only 28 cells, i.e. n=56, and its checks do not commute. No 56-cell lattice works either: 7×8, 8×7, 14×4, 4×14, 2×28 and 1×56 do not commute, and 28×2 and 56×1 give k=12 and 8.

Both rows are reproduced exactly (n=112, k=6 and k=16) as 2BGA codes over the order-56 group `G = (Z_7 × Z_4) ⋊ ⟨s_y⟩`, with the qubits being the group elements:
- X check `p`: L block `{g·p : g∈f}`, R block `{p·g : g∈h}`;
- Z check `p`: L block `{p·g^{-1} : g∈h}`, R block `{g^{-1}·p : g∈f}`.

In matrix form, `H_X = (L(f^T) | R(h^T))` and `H_Z = (R(h) | L(f))`, where L and R are the left and right regular representations. This is the Lin–Pryadko 2BGA form (ref. [84], cited in App. A), and also the `H = {e}` case of Construction 1 of arXiv:2606.17268 (the paper's ref. [82]; see `papers/new/2606.17268.md`).

The match is specific. Using L on both blocks fails to commute. The direct product `Z_7 × Z_4 × Z_2` gives k = (6, 14). Letting the mirror act on `Z_7`, or using `c_2`, gives k = (0, 2) and (0, 4). The paper never says that these two rows use the regular representation; this is our reading.

### Conventions that cannot change d

- Reading words left to right gives exactly the X↔Z dual code. I checked this row by row: the row spaces agree after `z ↦ −z` and a swap of blocks.
- Taking `X=(f^T p | h^T p)`, `Z=(h p | f p)` also gives the dual.
- For n=112, exchanging L and R gives the dual too.

So n, k and `d = min(dx,dz)` are fixed. The paper reports only `d`, which is why the builder sets `dx = dz = None`.

Shifting a mirror off the origin by one cell changes k for [[24,4,4]], [[40,14,4]], [[160,26,8]] and for the Fig. 4 codes. This confirms the origin-centred mirrors of Eq. (42). For odd L every mirror centre is equivalent anyway, by translation.

## Geometry and locality

Locators: Sec. IV B, Eqs. (43)–(47), Fig. 2; Sec. V, Fig. 3(b); Sec. VI D.

- **Folded lattice.** Cells are grouped into supercells, one per orbit of the point group. For a mirror, these are `{a}` on the mirror line and `{a,−a}` elsewhere. In the folded geometry a reflection is an intra-supercell move, translations move between neighbouring supercells, and a reflected axis becomes an open segment (Sec. IV B). The paper's locality metric is `R_fold`, the longest check–data edge with data and checks on two layers (Eq. (47)). It is illustrated only for [[36,4,6]] and not tabulated.
- **xy in the builder.**
  - **Folded axes.** An axis that some term reflects (and that has more than 2 cells) is placed in the accordion order 0, L−1, 1, L−2, … of Sec. V / Fig. 3(b). Mirror partners are then adjacent, the ends are open, and a translation by c moves at most 2|c| places. This is the uniform cell grid of Fig. 2(c), and also the placement Fig. 6 uses for its transmons.
  - **Other axes.** These stay periodic, at fundamental-domain positions 0..L−1, with the period in `inst["periods"]` and in the notes.
  - **Within a cell.** L-block qubits sit at the cell position and R-block qubits at +(0.5, 0.5), as in Fig. 1.
  - **Open variant.** `inst["xy_open"]` folds every axis, matching the paper's "two-axis accordion fold" of Sec. VI D.
- **Classification.**
  - **planar** (both axes folded, open boundaries): the C_2v rows of Table I ([[18,6,3]], [[24,4,4]], [[36,4,6]], [[40,14,4]], [[48,4,8]]) and Table III [[48,4,8]].
  - **chain** (1D, folded): [[14,6,2]], [[14,8,2]], [[32,6,6]].
  - **cylinder** (one folded axis, one periodic): all other C_s rows, both n=112 rows, Table II's reflection codes, Table III [[96,6,10]] and the Fig. 4 families.
  - **torus:** the BB codes, and [[96,8,10]]. In the latter, mod 2 the skew mirror `s` acts as `(x,y) ↦ (x, x+y)`, which stays within a column, so no fold is needed.
- **Locality is mostly trivial.** Every check has bounded weight, but on these small lattices the range of almost every Table I–III code is at or near the largest possible offset on at least one axis. Examples: [[32,6,6]] spans 15 of 16 places; [[84,10,9]] has x-range 7 on a ring of 14; [[160,26,8]] has 8 of a possible 10. Only the Fig. 4 families have a fixed range as L grows (≤ 6 places in folded x, ≤ 2 in y), which makes them genuinely 2D-local cylinder codes.
- **Tip, not used in `xy`.** For the periodic axis of a cylinder, relabeling `a ↦ u·a` with u a unit mod L is a code equivalence that commutes with the mirrors. It lowers the x-range for a few rows, e.g. [[132,8,11]] 9→6 with u=3, [[156,8,12]] 9→7 with u=11, [[120,14,12]] 7→6, [[42,6,6]] 3→2.

## Circuit-level information

- **No circuits.** The paper gives no gate-level syndrome circuit, no circuit-level noise model, no logical error rates and no circuit distances. Sec. VII lists circuit-level simulations, hardware-calibrated syndrome extraction and decoders adapted to the reduced translation symmetry as future work.
- **Neutral atoms (Sec. V; Sec. VI C, Eqs. (117)–(120), Fig. 5, Table II).** This is a coarse AOD movement model. Data qubits are fixed, and the X- and Z-check ancillas sit in two movable layers. Each ancilla visits the six data qubits of its weight-6 check in a cyclic order, e.g. `f_1→f_2→f_3→h_1→h_2→h_3→f_1`. The order is chosen to minimise `L_AOD = Σ W(v_{t+1} v_t^{-1})`, where `W` is the minimum longest single-array displacement over collision-free parallel moves. The resulting costs `L_m` are in the Table II section above. There is no CNOT ordering, X/Z interleaving or hook-error analysis.
- **Superconducting layout (Sec. VI D, Fig. 6, Table III).** The HAL routing of ref. [71] is applied to folded-lattice initial placements. The output is coupler-routing statistics only.

## Open questions and status of the distances

- **Status of the distances.**
  - Table I: all SAT-exact.
  - Table II: stated as "exact parameters", method not given.
  - Table III and Fig. 4: no method stated.
  - Nothing is presented as a bound or a conjecture.
- **Future directions flagged in Sec. VII.**
  - end-to-end fault-tolerant performance (circuit-level noise, calibrated syndrome extraction, decoders);
  - logical gates from qubit relabeling and code automorphisms (sketched in Sec. IV G);
  - extensions to nonsymmorphic space groups, higher dimensions, codes with defects, layered systems and multiblock CSS constructions.
- **Other limitations noted in the text.**
  - Theorem IV.5 (finite free module over an invariant polynomial subring) is only an existence result. The App. B table gives convenient choices, not a classification.
  - Point groups beyond reflections need not fold into a same-dimensional open region (Sec. IV B, ref. [45]).

## Recorded ambiguities and assumptions

1. **The n=112 rows.** The group label does not match n. The 2BGA reading above is ours; it reproduces n and k exactly and is the only reading found that does. If the authors meant something else, these two matrices could differ from theirs.
2. **Table III shapes.** The paper gives none. 3×8 for [[48,4,8]] and 12×4 for [[96,6,10]] are the unique factorisations with commuting checks and the stated k, and both agree with the 6×16 and 8×24 transmon arrays in Fig. 6(a),(f).
3. **dx versus dz.** These are not reported. Two natural conventions (word order; transposed template) swap them, so only `d` is comparable.
4. **[[12,8,2]].** The order (2,3) is taken from the group label, although 3×2 also gives k=8. On 3×2, `s_y` would act trivially (y mod 2), giving a plain BB code.
5. **Fig. 4 readings.** k and d are read off plots. Where lines overlap, the paper offsets them slightly. The k readings are confirmed by our rank computation at all 24 topological points; the d values are not checked.
6. **Fig. 4(c),(d) not reproduced.** The non-topological caption polynomials do not give the plotted k.
   - **Plotted:** code 1 has k = 16,10,24,14,32,18,40,22; codes 2 and 3 (drawn as one double line) have k = 8,6,12,8,16,10,20,12.
   - **Ours:** k = 12,6,20,8,20,12,24,12; 14,10,20,14,26,18,32,22; and 14,6,16,8,22,10,24,12.
   - **Partial agreement:** at odd L, our code 2 matches the plotted code-1 line, and our code 3 matches the code-2/3 line.
   - **What doesn't fix it:** mirror shifts, word order, transposing f or h, and swapping f with h all fail to reproduce it.
   - **Status:** unresolved; probably different polynomials, or curve labels permuted, in the figure.
7. **Fig. 4(a) upper bound for code 2.** The dashed line sits near 5, although all plotted k values are even. It is not used.
8. **d_kind judgment calls.** Table II is recorded as "exact" on the strength of the Fig. 5 caption wording alone. The gross code's d=12 is the established literature value (ref. [22]) but is recorded as "unknown", since this paper gives no method.
