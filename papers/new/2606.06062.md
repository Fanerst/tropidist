## What a barbell code is

A barbell code is a tile code; the paper calls barbell codes "a special case of tile codes" (main text). The barbell construction does three things (Supp. Sec. 3.2). It adds dummy stabilizers with empty support so that X and Z checks come in pairs. It places one ancilla per check. It translates each of the four qubit sublattices (X ancillas, Z ancillas, horizontal data, vertical data) by a fixed integer vector, so that each pair of checks lies inside a "barbell" of six hexagons on IQM's six-qubit star lattice (6QSL), with one near-local coupler (NLC) joining the two ancillas. None of these steps changes the stabilizer group. **The barbell code is the same code as the tile code** with the given tiles, layout and corners; only the hardware embedding differs. `hx` and `hz` in the module are the tile code's checks.

- The w6b family (second weight-6 row) at n = 288 is exactly a code already published in 2504.09171. Its tile pair equals the sixth of the eight [[288,8,12]] tile pairs drawn in that paper's Appendix B table, with the X and Z tiles exchanged; that paper counts the exchanged pairs among its 16 configurations. 2504.09171 says its distances were estimated with QDistRnd and then confirmed by integer linear programming; its Appendix B presents the 16 [[288,8,12]] configurations as results of that search. Cross-check: the parallel tile builder `papers/planar_tile.py` constructs this configuration independently from the tile paper's TeX as `tile_288_8_12_b6s`. Its X and Z row spaces equal those of `barbell_w6b_288_8_12`, with qubits matched by position (checked 2026-10-22). Per that module, the 16 Appendix B codes are a single code up to qubit relabeling.
- The w6a, w8 and w10 tiles are not among the tiles printed in 2504.09171. That paper's 4×4-box codes, [[288,18,13]] and [[512,18,19]], use different tiles.

## Tile-code layer: geometry and check matrices

Locators: Supp. Sec. 3.1, Eqs. (6)–(12); Methods, "Tile codes" (Steps 1–4); Fig. 3; Supp. Sec. 3.3, Table 3; 2504.09171, condition (T2) and the layout of its Fig. 1.

- Data qubits: q(h,u) is the edge {u, u+(1,0)} and q(v,u) the edge {u, u+(0,1)}, for u ∈ [0,L−1]×[0,M−1]. So n = 2LM; all instances have L = M.
- A tile is a set of h- and v-edges in the (D+1)×(D+1) box anchored at its lower-left vertex. Following the tile-code convention, the box contains no horizontal edge on its top line and no vertical edge on its right line. The X check at vertex u has support u + tile_X, truncated to existing qubits; likewise the Z check at u has support u + tile_Z. The Z ancilla sits on the plaquette u + (½,½).
- Check positions (Eqs. (9)–(12)): U_X,tile,base = [0, L−D−1] × [−D, M−1], i.e. bulk plus D truncated rows at the bottom and top; U_Z,tile,base = [−D, L−1] × [0, M−D−1], i.e. D truncated columns on each side. Corner checks are added as listed in Table 3.
- Step 4 (drop qubits that no X check or no Z check covers, then drop empty checks) removes nothing in any instance. The paper says so, and the module asserts it.
- Count: (L−D)(M+D) + (L+D)(M−D) checks plus the corners. All are independent in every instance, so k = 2D² − (number of corners).

Tiles. An entry (x,y) under h is the edge {(x,y),(x+1,y)}; under v it is {(x,y),(x,y+1)}. They were read from the vector paths of `weight6_1_tile.pdf`, `weight6_2_tile.pdf`, `weight8_1_tile.pdf` and `weight10_1_tile.pdf` (Table 3, column "Tile"). Every X/Z pair satisfies (T2) of 2504.09171: an X h-edge at (x,y) corresponds to a Z v-edge at (D−x, D−y), and vice versa.

## Barbell embedding and positions

Locators: Methods, "Barbell codes on the 6QSL plus NLC architecture" (hexagon vertex sets, the NLC offset (−1.5, 2.5) for w8); Supp. Sec. 3.2, Eqs. (13)–(27); Supp. Sec. 3.3, Eqs. (28)–(31); Fig. 5; Supp. Sec. 2, Eq. (5).

- Dummies and pairing (Eqs. (13)–(15)): the X anchor u pairs with the Z anchor u + v_XZ; a missing partner becomes a dummy. After translation, the X ancilla is at u (v_X = 0), its partner Z ancilla at u + v_XZ + v_Z + (½,½), and data qubit q(t,u) at translated index u + v_t. Each support moves along with its check (Eqs. (18)–(19)).
- Hexagons in the paper's square-grid coordinates: type A is (a,b) + {(0,0),(½,½),(1,1),(1,3/2),(½,1),(0,½)}, type B is (a,b) + {(0,0),(−½,−½),(−½,−1),(0,−½),(½,0),(½,½)}. Two qubits can interact if they share a hexagon. The neighbour sets of Eqs. (28)–(31) follow exactly from these hexagons; `check_barbell` recomputes both and asserts that they agree.
- Verification of the reading:
  - Both barbell conditions (every support qubit is adjacent to the check's own ancilla or to its partner's, never to both) hold for every check of all 27 instances.
  - The four barbell figures (`weight_*_barbell.pdf`) match the bulk barbells qubit for qubit (10 highlighted qubits each, with their X/Z membership).
  - For [[242,16,8]], Fig. 5 was parsed from its vector content. Both panels match the module exactly after one global offset: 121 h- and 121 v-qubit positions, 113 X and 113 Z checks, 49 + 49 dummies, and the supports of the two highlighted checks. Panel (a) is untranslated (`xy_tile`), panel (b) translated (`xy`).
- Position sets in each instance dict:
  - `xy`: the barbell layout in the paper's square-grid coordinates, where hexagons appear as slanted parallelograms.
  - `xy_chip`: the same lattice with regular hexagons of unit edge, as on the physical chip. Sublattice t with translated index w is placed at w₁(3,−√3) + w₂(−3/2, 3√3/2) + c_t, with c_X = 0, c_Z = (1,0), c_h = (3/2,−√3/2) and c_v = (−1/2,√3/2). This map is derived here, not given in the paper. It makes every type-A/B hexagon regular, gives the w8/w10 NLC length √139 ≈ 11.79 (Eq. (5): ≈ 11.8a), and reproduces the label geometry of Fig. 1(a) up to a 30° rotation. The weight-6 NLCs come out shorter, √31 for w6a and √79 for w6b, as Supp. Sec. 2 expects.
  - `xy_tile`: the untranslated edge midpoints.
  - `barbells` lists the ancilla pair of each barbell (rows of hx/hz, or None for a dummy), with anchors and ancilla positions.
- How each check splits over its two ancillas, counted as (qubits at a^X, qubits at a^Z) for bulk checks: w6a, w8 and w10 have w/2 + w/2 for both check types, as Supp. Sec. 4 assumes. **w6b does not**: its X checks split 4 + 2 and its Z checks 2 + 4. The same split is drawn in `weight_6_2_barbell.pdf`, so this is the paper's construction and not a reading error.

## Syndrome circuit

Locators: Methods, "Superdense syndrome extraction circuits" and Table 1 (w = 8); Supp. Sec. 4: Table 4 (general w), Remarks 1–2, Theorem 1, Lemma 2, Corollary 3, Eqs. (32)–(71); Fig. 1; Methods, "Numerical simulation".

- Cycle of Table 4 (superdense, after Gidney's color-code circuits):
  - Layer −1: RX on a^X, R on a^Z.
  - Layer 0: CNOT a^X → a^Z, using the NLC.
  - Layers 1…w/2: CNOT a^X → (X-check qubits at a^X) and a^Z → (X-check qubits at a^Z).
  - Layers w/2+1…w: CNOT (Z-check qubits at a^X) → a^X and (Z-check qubits at a^Z) → a^Z.
  - Layer w+1: CNOT a^X → a^Z.
  - Layer w+2: MX on a^X, M on a^Z.
  - The depth is w + 4: 12 for weight 8, and the paper's "depth surplus of 2" for weight 10. Within a layer, at most one interaction may use any one hexagon.
- **Weight-8 CNOT order (Fig. 1).** Fig. 1(b) gives the order as numbered labels 1–5 on the data around each ancilla, and Fig. 1(a) places those labels on the chip. Both panels were parsed from vector data (gate glyphs, connectors, text positions). The labels were then matched to lattice qubits by fitting the `xy_chip` geometry; all 10 labels land on model positions under one rotation. Offsets below are relative to the ancilla's translated index (u for a^X, u + v_XZ + v_Z for a^Z):
  - Labels at a^X: 1 = h(−1,0) (Z check only), 2 = v(−1,−1), 3 = h(0,0), 4 = v(1,1), 5 = h(0,1) (X check only).
  - Labels at a^Z: 1 = v(1,0) (X check only), 2 = h(1,1), 3 = v(0,0), 4 = h(−1,−1), 5 = v(0,−1) (Z check only).

  The module stores this as `W8_SCHEDULE`. `check_w8_schedule` runs on every w8 instance. On the boundary, gates on absent qubits are skipped, as the paper prescribes, and the schedule still covers every check's support exactly. No data qubit is used twice in a layer. A separate check (n = 242, 288, 450) found no hexagon used twice in a layer either. Each scheduled pair shares exactly one hexagon, so the mediating coupler is unique, and the X and Z ancillas alternate h/v qubits and A/B hexagons in every layer, as the Methods text states. In the bulk, every data qubit is busy in all eight CNOT layers. Per the Methods text, this order was the best of 100 random orderings in memory simulations.
- Weights 6 and 10: the paper gives no CNOT order. Remark 2 only asserts that orderings with one CNOT layer per step exist for all Table 3 families. For w6b, Table 4 does not apply as written, because of the 4 + 2 split.
- Frame and detectors (Theorem 1; Eqs. (45)–(54)). The paper shows that the outgoing value of S_i^X is m_i^X ⊕ Σ_{j∈B₁₁^Z(S_i^X)} m_j^X (Eq. (50)), and that U_frame (Eq. (51)) restores it to m_i^X. My own corollary, from Eqs. (45)–(49) together with Corollary 3 applied to S_i^X itself: the raw outcome m_i^X equals the value of S_i^X entering the round, and the data then carries a Z-type frame. In every bulk barbell of all four families, the barbell itself belongs to B₁₁. In the deep bulk, |B₁₁| is 6 for w8, 9 for w10, 5 for w6a and 6 for w6b, and smaller near the boundary. X-type detectors between rounds, the final X-basis data detectors and the X observables therefore need these extra record terms, or the frame must be applied as feedback. Z outcomes are plain (Eqs. (55)–(59)).
- Noise and experiments (Methods):
  - Noise is uniform depolarizing: reset and measurement flips with probability p, single-qubit depolarizing p on idles, two-qubit depolarizing p after each CNOT.
  - The memory experiment prepares |0̄⟩^⊗16 or |+̄⟩^⊗16, runs d rounds, then measures the data in the same basis.
  - Decoding uses Relay-BP-5 with R = 300 legs, up to 80 pre-iterations and 60 iterations per leg. X and Z are decoded separately, and the γ values are tuned for d = 11 and 14.
  - The LER per round is estimated from d rounds with at least 100 failures, then fitted to LER(p) = p^{d/2} exp(c₀ + c₁p + c₂p²).
  - Fig. 2(b) gives w8 memory LERs at p = 9×10⁻⁴ / 10⁻³: n = 242: 4.4e−5 / 7.9e−5; 288: 8.0e−6 / 1.7e−5; 338: 1.3e−6 / 2.4e−6; 392: 4.1e−7 / 8.8e−7; 450: 1.4e−7 / 2.1e−7. The logical-measurement experiment (n = 242) gives 7.4e−5 / 1.2e−4.
- **Circuit distance: not reported.** The paper gives no circuit-level distance, no fault-distance search and no hook-error analysis. The fit's exponent d/2 implicitly assumes effective distance d.
- **Can a Stim circuit be rebuilt?**
  - Weight 8: yes, from this extraction: the code, the barbell pairing, `W8_SCHEDULE`, Table 4, the frame-aware X detectors and the stated noise model. The paper does not say which qubits count as idle in the reset, Bell and measurement layers, or whether idles are charged per layer, so that must be chosen. Within the CNOT layers only boundary qubits ever idle.
  - Weights 6 and 10: an ordering must be chosen; for w6b, the cycle layout itself must be adapted.
  - The logical-measurement circuits (the Yang et al. protocol on an extended patch, Fig. 4) are not specified precisely enough to rebuild.
  - The paper publishes no circuit files.

## Recorded ambiguities and assumptions

- **Meaning of L.** Table 3's caption gives n = 2(L+D)(M+D). The definitions (Eqs. (6)–(7)), the Methods steps and Fig. 3 ("L = M = 11" for the 242-qubit code) all give n = 2LM. The caption's L is the number of bulk tiles per side. The corner positions (L−D, −D) and (−D, M−D) are corners only in the Eqs. (6)–(7) convention, which the module follows. With it, every row reproduces n.
- **Typos in Table 1.** In layers 1–4, both CNOTs of Table 1 target q_{i,r}^X; in layers 5–8, both are controlled by q_{i,r−4}^Z. Table 4 gives the intended q_{i,r+w/2}^X and q_{i,r}^Z, which also agree with Fig. 1(b).
- **Typos in the barbell conditions (Supp. Sec. 3.2).** Condition 1 writes u_XZ for v_XZ, and condition 2 drops v_XZ from the partner ancilla's index. The intended reading is unambiguous, and the module checks that reading.
- **Hexagon colours in Fig. 1(b).** In the barbell figures pink is type A. Layers 1–5 of Fig. 1(b) agree with the hexagon each pair necessarily uses; layers 6–8 have the two colours swapped. For example, the pair (a^X, label 4) is pink in layer 4 and cyan in layer 6, although that pair shares only one hexagon. This affects only hardware-level coupler bookkeeping. The gate order, read from the connectors, is unambiguous.
- **Split assumption.** Supp. Sec. 4 assumes a w/2 + w/2 split of every check over its two ancillas, which fails for w6b (see above). The proof of Theorem 1 does not depend on the split, but the layer counts of Table 4 do.
- **Tiles only in figures.** Table 3 gives the tiles only as pictures. They were read from exact vector coordinates and then confirmed independently: by (T2), by the barbell conditions with the paper's translation vectors, by the four barbell figures, and for w8 by the whole of Fig. 5.
- **Corners.** The w8 corners are confirmed by Figs. 3 and 5. The single Z corner of w6a, at (−D,−1), rests only on Table 3; it commutes with all X checks and gives the paper's k = 7. w10 uses the same corner rule as w8 (Table 3), confirmed by k = 16.
- **Distances.** See the distance-status paragraph above.

## Open questions flagged by the paper

From the closing paragraphs of the main text:

1. A decoder tailored to barbell codes may improve performance and run time.
2. Universal fault-tolerant computation by coupling tile codes to surface codes, shown for tile codes by Yang et al., still has to be verified with circuit-level simulation.
3. It is open which gates (for example H, S, T) are native to tile or barbell codes.

The paper also expects that better decoding could make the weight-10 family competitive despite its two extra layers. It does not expect crosstalk between the parallel NLCs to be a major obstacle (Supp. Sec. 2).
