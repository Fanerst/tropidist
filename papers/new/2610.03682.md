## Construction and conventions

The reconstruction uses the dual triangular tiling, rather than drawing the
trivalent color-code tiling. A smooth quotient of the reflection triangle
group has three nonidentity involutions `a,b,c`, with `ab,bc,ca` of orders
exactly `r,s,t`. Each group element is one triangular tile. In the regular
representation, right multiplication `g -> g*x` crosses the side marked
`x`. The base check supports are the right-action orbits of `<a,b>`, `<b,c>`
and `<c,a>`, of sizes `2r,2s,2t`. This agrees with Methods A and S1 A, D, F,
G. Checking exact orders excludes the reduced-order quotients that S12
warns may occur under the same parent triangle-group label.

For side length `L`, subdivide each base side into `L` segments and fill each
triangle with the ordinary triangular lattice. Small triangular faces are
qubits; fine-triangulation vertices are checks. The two copies of a base
side are glued by matching barycentric coordinates relative to its named
endpoints. A reflection fixes the side pointwise, so there is no reversal
of the named endpoints in this convention. Every check is used for both
Pauli types: `H_X = H_Z = H`. Every small triangle has three incident
checks. Interior vertices and inserted side vertices have valency six;
base corners retain their valencies `2r,2s,2t`.

For integer barycentric coordinates `(i,j,k)` with `i+j+k=L`, the refined
vertex color can be chosen as `i+2j (mod 3)`. It agrees across glued sides
and gives three different colors on every small triangle, including on
nonorientable surfaces. At `L = 0 (mod 3)`, all three base corners acquire
the same refined color; the refined color need not equal the original
corner type. The paper does not give an explicit refined coloring, but S2 A
allows all integer `L >= 1` and describes exactly this subdivision. The
check matrix does not require storing a color label.

The expected counts are equations S14–S16:

```
n = L^2 N
edges = 3 L^2 N / 2
checks = N/2 * (L^2 - 1 + 1/r + 1/s + 1/t)
k = n - 2 rank_F2(H) = 4 + N * (1 - 1/r - 1/s - 1/t)
```

The last formula is S13. It holds for both orientable and nonorientable
closed surfaces, as stated in S1 F and S2 A. Only for an orientable surface
does it become `k=4g`; on a nonorientable surface it is twice the crosscap
number. Orientability is equivalent to a consistent word-length parity on
the regular Cayley graph, with each generator reversing that parity.

Left multiplication `g -> h*g` is a global code symmetry and keeps the
local position within a triangle. Right multiplication by a fixed
generator is local adjacency and generally is not a global code symmetry.
This distinction is explicit in S1 G. The orientation-preserving subgroup
used by the paper for logical walking circuits is smaller than the full
left-action group usable for distance search.

S1 F iii defines the orientable cover as the subgroup of `G x Z2` generated
by `(a,1),(b,1),(c,1)`. Its order is `2N` for a nonorientable base, and `N`
for an already orientable base. Calling this construction `G x Z2` alone
does not justify doubling an orientable code.

## What the paper certifies

Distance means code-capacity Pauli distance here; no extraction circuit is
reconstructed. Self-duality makes the X and Z distances identical (S10 B).
The paper has two ways to certify an exact color-code distance: completed
exhaustive connected-cluster search, and agreement of the independently
computed restricted-lattice lower bound and monochromatic-string upper
bound, `d_res = d_string`. Thus connected-cluster search is not its only
exact method. S10/Table S5 restrict the cluster runs to `n <= 2000` with a
300-second timeout, while the bound methods also certify larger codes.
The separate timing benchmark in Figure S61 uses 600 seconds per code.

The lower bound is the median of the three restricted-lattice systoles,
not the shortest of them (S63). The upper bound is twice the shortest
shrunk-lattice systole (S54–S56). A branching logical can beat that upper
bound: the base Bolza code has exact distance two and string upper bound
four (Figure S59). An upper-bound witness or an unfinished exact search
must not be recorded as an exact distance.

## Identification limits and assumptions

The paper gives neither generator triples nor a reproducible numbering of
the LINS normal subgroups or epimorphisms. Its `_32`, `_40`, etc. are
enumeration labels, not group orders or SmallGroup IDs. The suffix `|1`
similarly cannot be mapped to a particular rebuilt triple from the text
alone. Matching `N`, orientability and `k` narrows candidates; matching
the certified distance sequence provides additional evidence and is
recorded separately from a proved identification.

Methods A says that `cbabca=e` holds for Bolza. It gives that relation as
an example from the boundary identifications, not as a complete finite
presentation. A reconstruction imposing it must independently verify
order 16 and the smooth `(4,4,4)` orders. The guess that `_96` is
`PGL(2,7)` is not stated by the paper and must be tested, not assumed.

The elementary-abelian-cover route is a reconstruction method proposed
for this task, not a method described in the paper. Face-closed voltages
and invariant cohomology subspaces generate candidates. Each returned
candidate still needs a direct regular-action and generated-order check;
only then is it a smooth normal quotient suitable for this registry. Cover
counts must say whether they count invariant subspaces, marked extensions
or inequivalent unmarked tilings. The paper's exhaustive LINS enumeration
is not reproduced merely by finding covers along the requested chains.

Epimorphism classes are taken modulo target-group conjugation and field
automorphisms as requested. For PSL targets, an additional automorphism
coming from PGL can relate distinct inner-conjugacy classes. Postcomposing
a surjection with any target automorphism leaves its kernel unchanged, so
the corresponding regular marked Cayley tilings can be isomorphic even
when these requested epimorphism classes are distinct. Permuting `a,b,c`
in a symmetric signature can also identify unmarked tilings. The notes
therefore distinguish epimorphism-class counts from tiling counts.

### Driver scope and remaining checks

`hyperbolic_run.py` accepts `out.json name [max_d] [seconds per threshold]`,
asserts self-duality and searches one basis. Checkpoints include the matrix
hash, threshold completion states and physical witnesses. Resume derives
its lower bound only from a consecutive sequence of exhausted thresholds.
Lookup tables are estimated and printed before allocation: use triples
when their estimated table fits 512 MiB, otherwise pairs when theirs fits
1 GiB, otherwise singles. A separate conservative process estimate must
fit the default 4-GiB ceiling. A singles-table estimate can also be refused.
This estimate is not a guarantee of measured RSS.

The driver honors `OMP_NUM_THREADS` and defaults it to two. Its default
whole-invocation budget is 570 seconds; `HYPERBOLIC_TOTAL_SECONDS=0`
removes this cap for a server run. For `n>2048`, redundant unpaired
nullspace labels avoid the standard paired-logical construction; for a
self-dual code this still tests exactly whether a zero-syndrome word lies
outside `row(H)`. The current search kernel accepts at most 16320 check
rows. The driver uses the exact rational S16 count to reject larger cases
before allocating the matrix or lookup table.
Consequently some registered large refinements require a kernel extension
before they can be searched, even on a larger server.

`.venv/bin/python tests/test_hyperbolic.py` ran and exited 1. All 30
tilings and 60 meshes passed its structural checks; all four Bolza exact
distances and the order-32 base distance passed. The next certified
comparison failed with `AssertionError: ('bolza32_L2', 6, 6, 'found')`,
where the expected paper distance was 8. The failure remains visible;
the test was not weakened to accept the rejected `_40` identification.

After strengthening the S15 check, a structural-only rerun passed all
30 tilings and 60 meshes in 22.887 seconds with peak RSS 145850368 bytes
(approximately 139 MiB). It performed no kernel or distance searches.
Actual outcomes are saved in `structure_checks.json`. The verified
structural scope is every base tiling and every refinement through `L=4`
for `N<=1344`; larger tilings get base-matrix checks only. Larger refined
`n,k` entries in the catalog and table are formula predictions until their
actual matrices are checked.

Structural checks covered involutions, transitive regular action, exact
rotation orders, dihedral face-orbit sizes, all three S14–S16 counts,
column weight three, `H H^T=0`, actual binary rank and every advertised
row/qubit symmetry. S15 now independently enumerates barycentric
microtriangle edges and glues matching base-side edges with the generator
permutations, retaining multiedges; it does not reuse the code-builder's
internal mesh. Separate checkpoint verification passed all eight saved
physical witnesses and consecutive exhausted-threshold certificates.
For the order-32 witnesses, an independent binary-rank check also found
`rank(H plus witness) > rank(H)` and zero syndrome.

Requirements to reproduce the paper's order-32 row and obtain an entirely
passing test are not met. The Bolza row is verified; the other requested
targets and cover chains are built with the structural scope stated
above. Their paper identities and new distances remain unresolved. No
larger distance run was initiated after the discrepancy, and no remote
job was started.
