# hypsurf: modular surface-code circuits of arXiv:2610.03682v1

Oscar Higgott et al., *Low-Overhead Quantum Error Correction with
Boundary-Connected Planar Modules*. This reconstruction reads the supplied
`2610.03682_fulltext.txt`: Methods C and F, Table M4, Supplement S1 B/E,
S2 B, S3 B, and S4 B i–vii. The supplied text has lost the figures; their
captions and equations remain available. No network, installation, GAP
execution, remote job, or change to the earlier color-code work is involved.

## Codes and provenance

The four base tilings come from the user's existing LINS output. Positions
in each JSONL file are one-based; the paper's subscript is position minus
one. A triangle is a point in a regular quotient of `triangle(2,4,q)`.
Its quadrilateral face is the orbit of `<b,c>`, its side the orbit of
`<a,b>`, and its vertex the orbit of `<c,a>`. Their orbit sizes are 8, 4,
and `2q`. Local face orientations are coordinate conventions, not extra
identifications of the tiling.

The face frame starts at the minimum triangle index in its orbit and
follows `c` then `b` to enumerate its four cyclic corners. Each vertex's
cyclic incidence order starts at its minimum triangle and follows `a`
then `c`. These choices fix the local NW, NE, SE, SW frames and the cat
chain order reproducibly. Data and local ancillas are indexed in
`face,y,x` order. Glued vertices are represented by their minimum local
vertex index, and global check indices are assigned in ascending order
of those representatives. Check zero starts with color X; the connected
fine-grid bipartite traversal determines all other colors. Choosing the
other initial color would exchange the names of the Pauli types.

Each base face is subdivided into an `L x L` square grid. Small squares
are data qubits; fine-grid vertices are checks. Shared base sides glue
matching boundary vertices. Fine-grid edges join neighboring vertices,
which are colored directly by a bipartite traversal.
The two colors give X and Z checks. A check inside a face or on an inserted
side vertex has weight four; a base corner retains weight `q`. This agrees
with S2 B. Bipartiteness is checked on the fine tiling, because the paper
explicitly warns that a closed base tiling can fail to be bipartite and
some even refinements can repair it. The requested L=1,3,5 cases are odd.

For `F` base faces, equations S21–S25 give

```
n = F L^2
fine edges = 2 F L^2
checks = F (L^2 - 1) + 4F/q
k = n - rank(H_X) - rank(H_Z) = 2 + F (1 - 4/q).
```

At L=1, different vertices can have identical check supports; these are
legitimate redundant checks and are retained. The code has one data qubit
per face, rather than one per edge of the primal tiling. The latter would
reconstruct the unrotated construction that the paper explicitly contrasts
with its construction.

## Schedules and cat-flagged extraction

A square module is one base face. Every face has one separate ancilla at
each of its `(L+1)^2` local grid vertices. Identified local vertices share a
check but keep their separate ancillas, including different occurrences
within one face if the tiling identifies boundaries. A bulk check uses
one ancilla, a side check uses two, and a base corner uses `q`. This gives
`F [L^2 + (L+1)^2]` total physical qubits, the square N column of S2.

For an X check the root starts in `|+>` and the other cat ancillas in
`|0>`; cat preparation uses CNOTs from the growing cat to its new neighbor.
Its local data gates are ancilla-to-data CNOTs. The inverse preparation
concentrates the syndrome onto the root, measured in X, and leaves the
other ancillas as deterministic Z flags. Z checks use the Hadamard dual:
root in `|0>`, others in `|+>`, all CNOT directions reversed, root measured
in Z, and X flags. A one-ancilla check is ordinary extraction. The text
requires a linear cat chain with preparation depth `ceil(m/2)` and inverse
decoding. It does not publish the chain cut, root choice, clockwise
orientation, or tie-breaking of the two middle roots.

The implementation cuts each ordered corner-incidence cycle before its
first incidence, takes the left middle ancilla `(m-1)//2` as the root, and
starts with a CNOT from that root to its right neighbor. Both fronts then
grow outward in parallel. A two-ancilla side check takes the first local
ancilla as root. These choices give precisely `ceil(m/2)` encoding layers
for `m>1`; `m=1` needs none. Decoding reverses the layer order. The chain
uses neighboring face incidences around the vertex, not a star joining
every ancilla directly to the root.

Each cat stage starts all participating chains together. Their encoding
layers are aligned at the beginning of preparation; their individually
reversed layers are aligned at the beginning of decoding. Thus a short
Bell chain completes its decoding while a long corner chain continues
through later inverse layers. Gates on disjoint checks commute in the
ideal circuit, but this alignment determines idle noise locations. The
text does not specify this choice.

The ZZ domain is the eight D4 images of `[0,1,3,2]` in cyclic corner
order. The NZ domain is the eight simultaneous D4 images of
`P_X=[3,1,0,2]`, `P_Z=[3,2,0,1]` at `[NW,NE,SE,SW]`. Translating that
corner schedule throughout a face fixes its local ancilla–data layers.
ZZ neighboring faces obey S31. NZ neighboring faces obey the corresponding
fine-code condition S26, rather than applying the same ZZ comparison to
both seed schedules. The code stores the direction from an ancilla to its
data qubit as NW, NE, SE, SW; the corner occupied by that ancilla in the
data square is the opposite direction. This 180-degree coordinate
convention keeps the same D4 domain. Square modules do not need the extra
same-module collision constraint S32. Every returned fine schedule is
checked for gate collisions and S26 directly. At L=1 a pair of checks can
overlap in more than two data qubits; validation uses the general even
parity of the effective cat-to-cat CNOTs instead of assuming a two-qubit
overlap.

The standard circuit repeats the four coupling layers in the same order.
The alternating circuit reverses only their order on odd physical cycles;
it keeps cat preparation before and cat decoding after coupling. The paper
states this reversal explicitly, including for multi-rate circuits.

In the multi-rate circuit, only checks at original base vertices are
subsampled. On cycles `t mod 3 = 0` their cats are prepared, on `=1` they
couple in the common extraction layers, and on `=2` they decode and
measure. Bulk and side checks, including side Bell checks, are fully
prepared, extracted, decoded, and measured every cycle. Corner syndrome
detectors compare measurements three cycles apart; ordinary checks compare
successive measurements; each flag is a separate deterministic detector.

## Noise and memory boundaries

Table M4 applies noise after transpilation to CZ gates, single-qubit
rotations, Z resets and Z measurements. In this gate set the fault channels
are `DEPOLARIZE2(p)` after an in-module CZ and
`DEPOLARIZE2(p_seam)` after a cross-module CZ, `DEPOLARIZE1(p/10)`
after a single-qubit gate or an ordinary idle, `X_ERROR(2p)` after a reset,
and `DEPOLARIZE1(p)` before a measurement whose reported bit flips with
probability `5p`. Qubits idle during reset or measurement receive
`DEPOLARIZE1(2p)`. The separate seam rate belongs only to cross-module
CZ locations; it is not assigned to all boundary qubits or operations.
Both probabilities must be nonzero to include their fault locations in a
distance problem.

The paper describes an indefinitely repeating three-cycle multi-rate
timeline, but Table S2 requests five rounds. Five physical cycles end with
a prepared and coupled corner cat, before its normal decoding cycle.
The finite-memory circuit therefore needs an explicit boundary convention;
the text gives neither a five-round Stim circuit nor the extra boundary
steps. Here, each requested cycle executes resets and all scheduled cat
preparation first, the four common coupling layers next, and the
scheduled decoding and measurement last. For five cycles, pending corner
cats are decoded and measured at coordinate round 5 before the final
data readout, whose closing detectors have round 6. This is a boundary
flush, not a sixth full extraction cycle. An uncoupled pending cat for
another requested round count is decoded and gives an absolute root
detector as well as flags; it does not replace the last true syndrome
measurement used for final closure.

Every ideal CNOT is compiled independently as `H(target), CZ,
H(target)`, without cancelling neighboring Hadamards. Each H layer, CZ
layer, reset layer, and measurement layer has its own tick. Spectator
qubits receive the Table M4 ordinary-idle or reset/measurement-idle
channel in the corresponding layer. The text does not specify whether
its transpiler cancels such Hadamards or the exact ordering of these
independent operations. These are recorded implementation choices, not
claims that the paper published these choices. No idle channel is added
to the zero-time annotation instructions.

Each memory experiment resets all data in the chosen basis and measures
it in that basis at the end. Initial known-basis stabilizers have an
absolute detector; the other stabilizers start with comparisons only after
their first measurement. All flags are absolute detectors from their
first measurement. Final data parities close the chosen-basis stabilizer
detectors, and independent logical supports supply `k` observables.

The detector convention includes local syndrome comparisons, all flags,
and initial/final known-basis checks. It does not add an extra detector
for a global dependency parity among the first unknown-basis check
measurements. Such a parity can be deterministic even though the
individual outcomes are unknown. This is an explicit detector-boundary
choice; the reconstruction does not claim to declare every possible
deterministic global parity of measurement records.

## Exact-pipeline interface

Detectors have four coordinates `(check,0,round,type)`. Their check index
belongs to the physical ancilla that produced the measurement, so distinct
flags and distinct roots cannot be merged by `collapse_time`. Types below
3 contain Z-check syndrome comparisons and X-check flags; these are the
detectors sensitive to X faults. Types 3 or above contain X-check syndrome
comparisons and Z-check flags, sensitive to Z faults.

For a Z memory use `sector_problem(full, high=False)`; for an X memory use
`sector_problem(full, high=True)`. At L=3 and L=5, both classes have flag
detectors in round zero, so the default `sector_rows` inference is
ambiguous. At L=1 all checks are corners, so the first multi-rate
measurement occurs in round 2; round-zero inference cannot be used there
either. The existing `papers/run_parallel.py` calls `sector_problem(full)` without
`high` and cannot pass it through its command line or module interface.
It was not changed. Use the explicit `high` argument when constructing a
sector problem for these circuits. Sector exactness must be checked on
the resulting undecomposed DEM before treating the sector reduction as an
exact-distance reduction.

The module provides `CODES`, `build`, `tag`, `matrices`, and
`translations`. Integer keys 6, 8, 10, 12 select the L=5 ZZ standard
code; explicit keys include `q6_L5_NZ_fb` and `q12_L5_ZZ`. The final
`_fb` selects the alternating coupling schedule. Explicit `schedule`,
`alternating`, and `assignment` arguments to `build` override the key's
schedule defaults. `translations` returns an empty list: the particular
cat-chain cuts and face schedule need not preserve generic tiling
automorphisms, and no unverified circuit symmetry is advertised.

The fourth field in `CODES[key]` records the paper's Table S2 upper bound.
It is not a newly certified distance for the default circuit or an
arbitrary override assignment. The actual graphlike bound depends on the
assignment and is recorded in the survey. For example, the default
`q8_L5_ZZ_fb` circuit has graphlike distance six, while its paper metadata
is eight. The default assignment was preserved; it was not changed to
match the table.

For example, construct a sector for an exact run with:

```python
from papers import hypsurf
from cdist import problem

key, basis = "q6_L5_NZ_fb", "Z"
circuit = hypsurf.build(key, basis, rounds=5, p=0.001, p_seam=0.001)
path = "results/papers4/hypsurf/my_memory.stim"
circuit.to_file(path)
full = problem.load_dem_problem(path, key)
sector = problem.sector_problem(full, high=hypsurf.sector_high(basis))
assert problem.sector_is_exact(full, sector)
collapsed = problem.collapse_time(sector)
```

The full undecomposed DEM retains non-graphlike faults for the exact
search. On the smallest {4,6}, L=1, five-cycle memory,
`sector_is_exact` passed in both bases: 48 full rows, 26 sector rows,
106 sector columns, and 12 collapsed rows. It also passed at L=3 with
round-zero flags in both classes: 408 full rows, 230 sector rows,
1,054 sector columns, and 48 collapsed rows. These counts use the
default ZZ standard assignment and positive bulk and seam rates.

## Verification and Table S2

The four specified regular quotient actions, the L=1,3,5 mesh counts,
check supports, bipartite coloring, CSS commutation, ranks, ancilla
incidences, and total square-module qubit counts passed direct checks.
The existing weight-bounded exact solver reproduced distances two at
L=1 and ten at L=5 in both bases for all four tilings. Its exhausted
thresholds and checked physical witnesses are saved in
`results/papers4/hypsurf/code_distances.json`. An independent exact
breadth-first search of the labeled syndrome graphs reproduced distances
two, six, and ten at L=1,3,5 in both bases, with separate physical
witnesses in `code_distances_independent.json`.

For the independent search, every data column has two syndrome vertices,
so a syndrome-free support is an even subgraph and splits into cycles. A
nonzero logical label requires at least one of those cycles to have odd
parity in a logical bit. For each bit, breadth-first search in its
two-sheeted graph cover finds the shortest walk from `(v,0)` to `(v,1)`;
minimizing over every vertex and every bit gives the exact distance. The
saved support has the corresponding weight, zero syndrome, and rank
increase over the stabilizer row space. This is an exact code-distance
certificate independent of Stim's circuit upper bound.

The number of valid labeled-face assignments is exactly `4^F`, for each
of ZZ and NZ: 4,096, 65,536, 1,048,576, and 16,777,216 for the four
tilings. These counts are computed by transfer matrices on the cyclic
face constraint graph, without materializing all assignments. The survey
can draw uniform distinct assignments using exact suffix counts; neither
distances nor the paper targets influence that sampling. Face symmetries
and global time reversal have not been quotiented out.

The square-module survey completed all 32 requested tiling, schedule,
coupling-order, and memory-basis rows, with 256 assignments in each row:
8,192 literal full-circuit Stim graphlike searches. The initial plain
test-script invocation with `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1` passed in 9.7 seconds.
The final required command, `.venv/bin/python tests/test_hypsurf.py`,
also passed; its total wall time was not recorded.
The verification environment was Python 3.9.6 and Stim 1.15.0 in the
existing `.venv`.
The test checks all 32 L=5, r=5 square variants: four tilings, ZZ/NZ,
standard/alternating, and X/Z memory. For its first valid assignment per
tiling and schedule, every detector and observable is deterministic,
every undecomposed one-fault mechanism that flips a logical has a
detector, total qubit counts match Table S2, and the four sector checks

The circuit values in S2 are upper bounds found with
`stim.Circuit.shortest_graphlike_error`, not exact circuit-distance
certificates. A code distance of ten does not certify a circuit distance
of ten. Schedule assignments are surveyed without selecting a circuit
because it matches a target; the saved distribution must state its
coverage and reproducible sampling convention.

No exact circuit-distance search was run. In particular, a graphlike
value of nine does not rule out a shorter fault set involving a
non-graphlike mechanism. The full undecomposed DEM and explicit memory
sector are the inputs prepared for the user's exact server runs.

The installed Stim call defaults to `ignore_ungraphlike_errors=True`.
This discards mechanisms with more than two detectors during the
graphlike upper-bound search. Canonicalizing the explained physical
faults only changes their presentation. Using
`ignore_ungraphlike_errors=False` would instead decompose some faults;
the installed documentation warns that decomposed terms without a
matching individual physical fault need not give an upper bound on
physical circuit distance. The survey retains the default semantics.

Every surveyed assignment uses the full
`Circuit.shortest_graphlike_error(ignore_ungraphlike_errors=True,
canonicalize_circuit_errors=True)` call in both memory bases and for
both standard and alternating coupling orders. The circuit retains the
full Table M4 noise model. No detector-sector acceleration or hyperedge
decomposition is used to obtain the reported distributions.

`papers/hypsurf_table.py` saves a JSON checkpoint after bounded batches
and resumes the same sample when rerun. The default requests 256 uniform
distinct assignments per tiling and schedule, using seed 382; a local
invocation stops after 240 seconds. Its `covered` field is the number
actually searched in a row, while `sampled` records the number drawn;
these must not be confused. For example:

```
OMP_NUM_THREADS=2 .venv/bin/python papers/hypsurf_table.py \
  results/papers4/hypsurf/table_s2_square.json 256 240
```

The optional arguments are `out samples seconds q seed kind basis`, with
defaults `samples=256`, `seconds=240`, `q=all`, `seed=382`, and
`kind=all`, `basis=all`. The q argument limits an invocation to one tiling;
kind may be `all`, `ZZ`, or `NZ`, and basis may be `all`, `X`, or `Z`.
This supports independently checkpointed split jobs,
for example:

```
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 .venv/bin/python papers/hypsurf_table.py \
  results/papers4/hypsurf/q6_nz.json 256 240 6 382 NZ
```

An unlimited time budget, specified as zero seconds, is for a server.
`complete_for_selection` describes completion of the requested
q/kind/basis subset. `complete_for_selected_q` requires both schedule
kinds and both bases for each selected tiling, even when an invocation
requests a smaller subset.
Before resuming, the driver checks the recorded Stim version and the
canonical SHA256 hash of the first saved example circuit in each row.
It rejects a changed Stim version or changed constructor fingerprint
before appending graphlike results. The final field name is
`example_circuit_sha256`; the earlier `example_matrix_sha256` spelling
is accepted when reading older checkpoints. Noise parameters and
multi-rate settings are pinned explicitly to the recorded Table S2
configuration. A completed all-row resume rechecked all 32 constructor
hashes and made no additional graphlike calls; the total remained 8,192.
Rows report the histogram, the paper's value, whether that value occurs
and is a mode, all individual distances in sample order, and a first
example circuit. That example is selected by sample order, not by its
distance. Initial example circuits need not reproduce the paper's value.

## Scope left open

Rectangle modules, matching choices, ZZ shifts, NZ same-module
translational invariance, and boundary-first fallback are outside the
initial square reconstruction. They require different ancilla counts and
collision constraints. They must not be represented by changing only
the square-module schedule. Rectangle circuits were not built or surveyed;
none of Table S2's rectangle rows is claimed to be reproduced. Circuit
files from the authors are unavailable, so even
a matching graphlike value cannot identify their unpublished assignment,
cat-chain convention, transpilation, or finite-time boundary convention.
