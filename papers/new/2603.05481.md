# Gross-code left-right extraction: arXiv:2603.05481v2

Scope: printed paper definitions and numerical figure labels only. No author
repository, deposited Stim file, Zenodo data, or executable source was fetched.
The assumption is that numerical labels in printed Figures 10 and 12 satisfy
the task's paper-text-only restriction. All coordinate choices below are
explicit relabellings of those drawings, not schedule optimization.

## Paper locators and claims

- Section IV.3 identifies the gross code as [[144,12,12]], with weight-six checks.
- Theorem 1, Eqs. (9)-(12), identifies non-interleaved circuit distance with
  the extended-code problem containing single-data errors and every check's
  residual errors. Section III.1 defines staggered left-right operation.
- Appendix H.1, Proposition 5: uniformly tiled non-interleaved circuits have
  distance at most 10. No explicit best uniform schedule is printed, so there
  is no `uniform` instance in `papers/lrgross.py`.
- Appendix H.1, Figure 11: three-colour residual patterns; cyclic colour
  permutations are allowed. Appendix H.2 and Figure 12, printed page 21:
  explicit schedule, period/depth 8, distance 11 conjecture, not an exact proof.

## Figure 10 geometry and base matrices

Use a 24-by-12 periodic drawing grid, with horizontal coordinate increasing
rightwards and vertical coordinate increasing downwards. Drawn X check
`(i,j)` is at `(2i,2j)`; drawn Z check `(i,j)` is at `(2i+1,2j+1)`.
There are 12-by-6 checks of each type. Hollow data circles are at `(2i+1,2j)`;
filled data circles are at `(2i,2j+1)`. The four nearest neighbours are N/E/S/W.
The two diagonal edges shown for one check of each type in Figure 10 have grid
displacements:

| Check | NE | SW |
| --- | --- | --- |
| X | (+6,-3) | (-3,+6) |
| Z | (+3,-6) | (-6,+3) |

The other diagonal edges follow the printed translation rule. These endpoints
were independently checked from the arXiv Figure 10 SVG path coordinates and
from the printed PDF image.

For the standard BB indexing used by `stabcode.bb_matrices`, sites are
`6*i+j`, with `i mod 12`, `j mod 6`; data blocks L/R have offsets 0/72.
The relabelling from the drawing is:

- Hollow drawn data `(i,j)` -> standard R `(i+2,j)`.
- Filled drawn data `(i,j)` -> standard L `(i,j+2)`.
- Drawn X check `(i,j)` -> standard X row `(i,j)`.
- Drawn Z check `(i,j)` -> standard Z row `(i+2,j+2)`.

Consequently the standard matrices are
`Hx=[A|B]`, `Hz=[B^T|A^T]`, with
`A=x^3+y+y^2`, `B=y^3+x+x^2`, `x^12=y^6=1`.
Here a positive term is the corresponding displacement of a row's data site;
transpose negates it. Thus the six directions have standard supports:

The cited base-code paper arXiv:2308.07915v2, Section 4, Eq. (2) and
Table 3's [[144,12,12]] row independently give these matrices, polynomials,
and periods 12 and 6.

| Direction | X row (i,j) | Z row (i,j) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| N | L(i,j+1) | R(i,j-2) |
| E | R(i+2,j) | L(i-1,j) |
| S | L(i,j+2) | R(i,j-1) |
| W | R(i+1,j) | L(i-2,j) |
| NE | L(i+3,j) | L(i,j-3) |
| SW | R(i,j+3) | R(i-3,j) |

The hollow/filled distinction is not the standard L/R naming: hollow is R,
filled is L. The figure's top-left X check and first Z check are red. In drawn
coordinates both have colour `(i+j) mod 3`, assigning R/G/B=0/1/2. In standard
row coordinates this becomes X `(i+j) mod 3`, Z `(i+j-4) mod 3`.

The whole printed circuit preserves translations `(di,dj)` with
`(di+dj) mod 3 = 0`. Of the 72 BB translations, 24 satisfy this condition,
including identity; `translations` returns the 23 nonidentity permutations.
The code itself admits all 71 nonidentity translations. These counts are
coordinate derivations; search-side survival is verified separately by the run.

## Figure 12 numerical schedule

Phases are 1-based modulo 8. The table transcribes the printed numbers; direction
order is N, E, S, W, NE, SW. Columns in the printed drawing are B, R, G; the
implementation stores R, G, B. X CNOTs point ancilla -> data; Z CNOTs data ->
ancilla (Section II.1).

| Type | Colour | N | E | S | W | NE | SW | Preparation | Measurement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Z | B | 4 | 8 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 3 | 2 | 1 |
| Z | R | 4 | 6 | 2 | 7 | 5 | 3 | 1 | 8 |
| Z | G | 4 | 7 | 2 | 5 | 6 | 3 | 1 | 8 |
| X | B | 2 | 1 | 4 | 8 | 3 | 7 | 6 | 5 |
| X | R | 2 | 8 | 1 | 7 | 3 | 6 | 5 | 4 |
| X | G | 2 | 7 | 1 | 6 | 3 | 8 | 5 | 4 |

Preparations are |0> for Z ancillas, |+> for X ancillas; measurements use the
matching basis. The lower panel's heading says X-check CNOT residuals, but the
caption and preparation/measurement table identify its numerical labels as
CNOT times. Treating that heading as a typographical error is the reading used.

A check starts at its preparation phase. For each printed gate/measurement
phase `s`, its absolute time in logical round `r=0,...,rounds-1` is
`reset + 8*r + ((s-reset) mod 8)`. The gate order therefore wraps through 8 -> 1:

| Type | Colour | Chronological gate directions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Z | B | SW, N, S, W, NE, E |
| Z | R | S, SW, N, NE, E, W |
| Z | G | S, SW, N, W, NE, E |
| X | B | SW, W, E, N, NE, S |
| X | R | SW, W, E, S, N, NE |
| X | G | W, E, SW, S, N, NE |

Sorting the printed integers would give the wrong X hook suffixes. Every
check has six consecutive CNOT times between its preparation and measurement.

## Memory boundaries and fault model

Section II.3 specifies independent operation faults: uniformly nonidentity
Pauli after an idle, uniformly nonidentity two-qubit Pauli after a CNOT,
preparation flips, and measurement flips. `noise="full"` uses DEPOLARIZE2 after
every CNOT, DEPOLARIZE1 on qubits idle in each layer, the appropriate flip after
resets, and the appropriate flip before ancilla measurements. Initial data
resets also receive preparation flips. `noise="noidle"` removes only idle
channels and is an explicitly named comparison model.

Section II.3 and Appendix E specify product-state data preparation, noisy
syndrome cycles, and a perfect final syndrome round reconstructed by data
measurement in the memory basis. The implementation's final data measurement
is therefore noiseless. It measures all k logical observables obtained from
`stabcode.css_logicals` in that basis.

Finite boundaries are not explicitly drawn in Figure 12. The chosen boundary
completion prepares all data first, starts each check at its first printed
preparation phase (Z at 1/2, X at 5/6), runs exactly `rounds` complete checks of
each type, and measures data after the last X measurement at phase
`8*rounds+5`. The bulk period remains 8; there are no partial checks at the
boundaries. This is an implementation assumption and is recorded for review.

## Relation to the effective problem

This Stim schedule shares X/Z layers on disjoint data blocks and staggers
ancillas; it is not `seq_circuit`'s literal X-stage-then-Z-stage cycle. Its
per-check relative order and non-interleaving satisfy the paper's Theorem 1.
`effective(key,basis)` applies `design.effective_problem` to the chronological
orders above. That is a use of the theorem, not an assumption that the timing is
sequential. `verify_effective` compares the effective column set against the
actual staggered circuit's time collapse and interior one-round windows;
verification outcomes belong in the test/run results, not this extraction.
