# Bivariate-bicycle surface (BBS) codes — extraction from arXiv:2606.08771

Source: R. Wang and L. P. Pryadko, *Algebra of Bivariate-Bicycle Surface Codes*, arXiv:2606.08771 (TeX source `svcode31.tex`, PDF dated June 9, 2026). This file records the definitions used by `papers/planar_bbsurface.py`. No author code or data was available. Only this paper was consulted. Equation, statement and example numbers follow the PDF.

## Lattice and check matrices

Locators: Sec. II C, Eqs. (4)–(7); Sec. II E, Eqs. (12), (15)–(16), Fig. 1; Sec. III, Eqs. (17)–(19), (21), Lemma 1.

- The codes are two-block CSS codes with `H_X = (A | B)` and `H_Z^T = (B ; -A)`, where `A`, `B` are the group-algebra matrices of two Laurent polynomials `a`, `b` (Eq. (12)). Every code in the paper is binary.
- On the plane (Fig. 1), X checks sit on vertices, Z checks on plaquettes, and qubits on horizontal edges (the `A` block) and vertical edges (the `B` block). Written out:
  - The X check at vertex `p` acts on `h(p-s)` for `s` in supp `a` and on `v(p-s)` for `s` in supp `b`.
  - The Z check at plaquette `p` acts on `h(p+s)` for `s` in supp `b` and on `v(p+s)` for `s` in supp `a`.
  - The module draws `h(i,j)` at `(i+1/2, j)` and `v(i,j)` at `(i, j+1/2)`.
  - The tiles drawn in Figs. 1, 2, 3, 5, 6 and 7 all match this convention exactly. Fig. 8 does not (see the ambiguities below).
- `d_X` is the minimum weight of a vector in `C(H_Z)^perp \ C(H_X)`, i.e. of an X-type logical (Eq. (7)). This is `dx` in `planar_common`.
- Bulk condition (Lemma 1): `a` and `b` are coprime. The common code dimension of a family is `k_TO = dim F[x^±1, y^±1]/<a,b>` (Eq. (21)). It counts the common roots with both coordinates finite and non-zero, with multiplicity (Appendix A).
- Edge conditions, which forbid localized edge logicals:
  - horizontal strips: `gcd(a_min(x), b_min(x)) = gcd(a_max(x), b_max(x)) = 1` (Eq. (39));
  - vertical strips: the same with `x` and `y` exchanged (Eq. (41));
  - tilted strips: the same in transformed coordinates (Eqs. (35), (45)).
  - Here `min`/`max` are the lowest and highest coefficient polynomials across the strip.
  - The module checks all four conditions for each of the 41 Table II pairs. All hold in the orientations used (rectangular: x and y; rotated: the two diagonals), consistent with Sec. V D.
- Balanced boundaries (Eqs. (37)–(38), (46)–(49)): the rows of vertical edges are shifted against the rows of horizontal edges by the degree differences `delta^min`, `delta^max` of `a` and `b` across the strip.

## Algorithm 1 as implemented

Locators: Sec. IV B, Eq. (34); Sec. IV E, Statement 7; Sec. V, Algorithm 1, Eqs. (45)–(49) and (46'), (48'); Sec. V B.

1. Regions. A strip is `R(m, L) = {0 <= m.(i,j) < L}` (Eq. (34)), applied to horizontal edges as in (46')/(48').
   - The vertical edges of the smooth strip `R1` are those of the plaquettes whose horizontal edges all lie in `R1`.
   - The vertical edges of the rough strip `R2` are those of the vertices whose horizontal edges all lie in `R2` (the paragraph after Eq. (49)).
   - For large `L` these unions are exactly the intervals of Eqs. (47) and (49).
2. Step 1. `S1X` is every vertex check with at least one edge in `R1`, truncated to `R1`. `S1Z` is every plaquette check inside `R1`.
3. Step 2. `S2Z` is `S1Z` truncated to `R1 ∩ R2`. `S2X` is the subgroup of `S1X` supported in `R1 ∩ R2`, a true shortening.
   - It is computed by elimination over a window of uncut vertex checks reaching `L1 + L2 + 4D` beyond `R2`. This follows the paper's warning to use only uncut generators.
   - Tripling the window changes nothing for any instance.
   - The extra elements, beyond the truncated vertex checks, are reduced greedily in weight.
4. Step 3. Drop the qubits outside `supp(S2X) ∩ supp(S2Z)`.
5. Orientation.
   - Regular (rectangular) codes: `m1 = (0,1)` is smooth with `L1 = L_y`, and `m2 = (1,0)` is rough with `L2 = L_x`.
   - Rotated codes: smooth strip `0 <= i+j < L1`, rough strip `0 <= i-j < L2`.
6. Tile construction (Sec. V B, needed for Example 3). Z checks are as above. X checks are the vertices whose edges all lie in `R2`, truncated to `R1`.

- I searched every `L1, L2 <= 30` (the range given in Sec. V D). Every row has at least one size with exactly the paper's `n` and `k`.

## Circuit-level information

The paper contains no syndrome-extraction circuits, no noise model, no circuit-level distances, no logical error rates and no thresholds. Its only remarks on circuits:
- Sec. I: BB codes measured with one ancilla per check give a torus connectivity graph with two long-range edges per qubit.
- Secs. V C and VI: lattice grafting is avoided because irregular generators complicate circuits.
- Sec. VI: when edge checks are trimmed bulk checks (all tile codes and many BBS codes), measurement schedules can be time-optimal like the surface code's, reusing the BB addressing pattern.

All Table II codes at their default sizes, and all examples except Examples 5 and 6, have checks of weight at most 6 that are truncations of the weight-6 bulk checks. The exceptions:
- the two k = 13 rows with an extra weight-4 corner X check;
- Example 5's weight-38 ladder;
- the weight-8 bulk checks of the weight-4 pairs in Examples 5 and 6.

## Open points flagged by, or found in, the paper

- **Distances are not certified.** Every `d` comes from vecdec (Sec. V D). The only proven statement is the lower bound of Statement 7, `d_X >= L_y/Delta_y` and `d_Z >= L_x/Delta_x`, for rectangular codes under the edge conditions. The conclusion claims `O(n^{1/2})` growth.
- **No guarantee of low-weight generators.** Sec. V C notes that the Gauss-elimination shortening need not yield sparse checks. Example 5's weight-38 ladder is the instance, and the paper replaces it by promoting a weight-6 Z logical.
- **Finite-size effects.** Sec. V D says `k < k_TO` occurs for small regions; the table rows are at sizes where `k = k_TO`.
- **Internal inconsistencies:**
  - Example 5's promoted code is `14/16` in the text and `14/14` in the Fig. 7 caption.
  - Example 6 says 16 weight-6 X logicals; only 15 translates of the weight-6 edge operator fit inside the region.

## Recorded ambiguities and assumptions

- **Region sizes.** Table II omits `L1`, `L2`, so 53 of the 68 rows are ambiguous (see above). The module's default per row is a heuristic. The alternates are exact Algorithm-1 codes with the same `n`, `k` and polynomials. A distance computation is needed to see which of them has the paper's `d`; possibly several do, and the authors' choice cannot then be recovered.
- **Rotated strip parity.** For diagonal strips the sign convention of the normal matters, because `i+j` and `j-i` have equal parity.
  - Sec. V A writes the rough normal as `m2 = (-1,1)`. With strips `0 <= i+j < L1` and `0 <= j-i < L2`, rows (7,11), (9,8), (9,9) and (11,4) cannot be matched for any `L1, L2 <= 30`.
  - With `0 <= i-j < L2`, all 40 rotated rows match. So do the Fig. 4 counts (56 X / 56 Z) and the monotone Fig. 8 family.
  - I tested 8 natural conventions: two diagonal assignments times two normal signs, times `a, b` as printed or swapped. Only this one fits every row; the others miss 4 to 7 rows.
- **Fig. 8 is drawn as a dual.** Its drawn tiles correspond, in the Fig. 1 convention, to `(b(1/x,y), a(1/x,y))` rather than to the caption's `(a, b)`. Its 48 X / 45 Z checks are exactly the CSS dual, reflected in `y`, of `bbs_fig8_103_10_5` (45 X / 48 Z), checked by explicit isomorphism.
  - The figure is therefore consistent with the module up to exchanging `d_X` and `d_Z`.
  - A reading with the drawn tiles taken literally (smooth normal `(-1,1)`) also matches 39 of the 40 rotated rows. It fails at (11,4,75), which is why it was not adopted.
  - For those 39 rows it gives the CSS duals of the module's codes, so `d = min(d_X, d_Z)` is unaffected.
- **Regular orientation.** Smooth horizontal strip and rough vertical strip, fixed by Examples 5 and 6: swapping the orientation turns 292 into 314 and back. `n` alone cannot fix it, since all four orientation and swap variants fit the 28 regular rows.
- **True shortening window.** The paper does not give `L_buf`. Window size `L1 + L2 + 4D` beyond `R2`; tripling it changes no instance.
- **Positions.** `xy` uses the raw lattice coordinates of Fig. 1 with the polynomials as printed (each contains the monomial 1). This fixes where the vertical-edge sublattice sits relative to the horizontal one.
  - Rotated codes are diamonds in this frame.
  - Each instance carries `normals` (smooth and rough strip normals) and `L1`, `L2`, for sweeps along the boundary directions.
- **Example 5 and 6 variants.** The promoted edge operators are built from the reduced boundary polynomials of the dual of Eq. (33): `a'_max = 1+y+...+y^4` with `b'_max = y`, and the `x`-analogue. All 16 (Example 5) and all 15 (Example 6) translates are mutually degenerate, so the added check's position does not matter. The tile-code fix of Example 3 uses the codeword drawn in Fig. 5; it is a different code from Algorithm 1's `[[50,7]]`, which instead gains a weight-2 corner X check.
